## 1. WHAT VISTRA ACTUALLY IS

Vistra is the largest competitive power generator in the United States — a vertically integrated independent power producer (IPP) that both **generates electricity** and **sells it directly to end customers** through its retail business. It is not a regulated utility. It earns market prices for its output, not government-set tariffs.

### The core business logic
Vistra owns roughly **41,000 MW** of generation across natural gas, nuclear, coal (being retired), solar, and battery storage. It also serves approximately **5 million retail customers** across 18 US states plus DC (brands: TXU Energy, Ambit Energy, Value Based Brands, etc.).

The retail business is strategically critical: it provides a **natural hedge** in ERCOT (Texas). When wholesale power prices spike (summer heat, winter storms), the Texas generation segment earns massive margins — but Vistra is simultaneously selling power to retail customers at contracted rates, which offsets volatile retail costs. This hedge relationship is why Vistra's ERCOT earnings are more stable than pure merchant generators.

## 2. THE NUCLEAR FLEET — VISTRA'S CROWN JEWEL

VST operates **~6.4 GW of nuclear capacity** across four sites:

| Plant | State | Market | Capacity |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Comanche Peak** | Texas | ERCOT | ~2,400 MW |
| **Beaver Valley** | Pennsylvania | PJM | ~1,800 MW |
| **Davis-Besse** | Ohio | PJM | ~908 MW |
| **Perry** | Ohio | PJM | ~1,268 MW |

### Why nuclear matters so much

**a) Economics:** Nuclear plants have near-zero fuel costs and very low marginal costs (~$20-25/MWh), so they capture nearly all of the wholesale power price as margin. At $60-80/MWh market prices, nuclear EBITDA margins are extremely high.

**b) IRA Section 45U Nuclear PTC:** The Inflation Reduction Act created a production tax credit for existing nuclear of up to **~$15/MWh** (phases down above ~$43-47/MWh power price). This effectively puts a floor under nuclear economics. UBS estimated VST's annual benefit at **$700-900mn in EBITDA** from this credit.

**c) Hyperscaler demand:** AI datacenters need 24/7 "always-on" power. Nuclear is the only large-scale carbon-free 24/7 source that can supply this. VST's nuclear fleet is therefore uniquely positioned to sign long-term Power Purchase Agreements (PPAs) with hyperscalers at material premiums to merchant prices.

### Nuclear PPA Pipeline — 3.8 GW Contracted, 3.2 GW Remaining

| Deal | Counterparty | Capacity | Duration | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Comanche Peak PPA** | AWS/Amazon | 1,200 MW | 20-year | Signed; starts Q4 2027 |
| **PJM Nuclear PPA** | Meta | 2,176 MW existing + 433 MW uprates | 20-year | Signed Jan 2026; deliveries begin late 2026, fully online 2034 |
| **Total contracted** | | **~3.8 GW** | | |
| **Remaining available** | | ~3.2 GW | | TBD (Beaver Valley + Comanche uprates) |

GS estimated the Meta/AWS-type deals are priced at **$85-100/MWh**, versus ~$60-70/MWh merchant power prices. That $20-35/MWh uplift on 3.8 GW = **$660mn-$1.3bn annual incremental EBITDA** when fully operational.

## 3. STRATEGIC GROWTH INITIATIVES

### a) Helix Digital Infrastructure (announced June 10, 2026 — most recent catalyst)
- New company designed as a **"one-stop shop"** for AI data center power needs
- Anchor investors: **KKR, Kuwait Investment Authority (KIA), NVIDIA, and VST**
- **$10bn committed capital** at launch
- Led by **Adam Selipsky**, former CEO of Amazon Web Services
- VST role: preferred power provider, can commit **up to $1bn capital** (milestone-linked; grows if successful)
- VST participates in both **GP and LP economics** (upside beyond just power sales)
- Key purpose: de-risks new generation development ($3bn per 1 GW new gas) by providing institutional capital; VST uses existing assets to serve initial demand
- UBS: *"directly refutes the narrative of BYOG [Build Your Own Grid] or BTM [behind-the-meter] new build as the only solution"*
- GS: *"could meaningfully expand VST's addressable market and act as a capital-light growth vehicle"*

### b) Cogentrix Acquisition (announced March 2026; expected close H2 2026)
- Acquiring **5,500 MW of natural gas generation** in PJM/Northeast
- UBS Cogentrix EBITDA estimates: **$211mn FY2026E → $570mn FY2027E → $601mn FY2028E**
- Strategic rationale: adds capacity in PJM exactly where load growth is strongest (AI datacenters clustering in Virginia/Pennsylvania/Ohio)
- New build gas at $2,500-3,000/kW implies $100+/MWh LCOE; current PJM energy + capacity prices ~$77/MWh → no new merchant entry viable, so Cogentrix's existing permits/interconnection are valuable
- Adds to the ~3.2 GW of uncontracted nuclear that Cogentrix customers could potentially pair with

### c) Meta PPA (January 9, 2026)
- **2,600 MW** across Beaver Valley (PA), Davis-Besse (OH), and Perry (OH)
- 20-year term, deliveries begin late 2026, fully ramped by 2034
- Estimated price: ~$85-100/MWh
- Adds ~6% EBITDA accretion in 2027 if full 2,176 MW delivered in year one; ~1% if evenly ramped to 2034
- GS did not embed this in base case at January 2026 announcement; now reflected in March 2026+ models

### d) AWS/Amazon PPA
- **1,200 MW from Comanche Peak** (Texas), 20-year term
- Starts Q4 2027
- Contributes ~25% FCF accretion annually at full ramp (per management)

### e) Uprate Opportunities (under analysis)
- **200 MW uprate at Comanche Peak** (Texas nuclear)
- **300 MW generation output enhancements** at PJM gas fleet
- Could expand contracted capacity and total generation output without new plant construction

## 5. REGULATORY & MARKET ENVIRONMENT

### ERCOT (Texas) — ~40% of VST earnings
- **Energy-only market**: no capacity payments, pure energy price signals
- VST is the largest generator in ERCOT with Comanche Peak nuclear plus multiple gas peakers
- Texas Senate Bill 6 (SB6): requires new dispatchable generation; incentivizes gas and nuclear investment
- **ERCOT batching vote (June 1, 2026)**: regulates how large AI datacenter loads connect to the grid
- Load growth: management guided to **3-5% annual peak load growth through 2030** (down from prior 5-6%)
- Structural challenge: ERCOT has no capacity market, so revenue can be lumpy year-to-year

### PJM (Mid-Atlantic/Midwest) — ~50% of VST earnings
- Capacity market: generators paid for being available (reliability payments in addition to energy)
- June 2024 PJM capacity auction cleared at **$269.92/MW-day** — approximately 9x the prior year, signaling severe supply tightness
- **FERC/PJM co-location rules (expected order ~June 18, 2026)**: defines whether data centers can be placed directly behind nuclear plants on-site. Critical for Beaver Valley contracting discussions
- **Cogentrix** positions VST in PJM ahead of massive load growth in Virginia/Pennsylvania/Ohio
- Credit rating: VST is **Ba1 (corporate family)/BBB-**; management expects upgrade to investment grade in 2026

### IRA Section 45U Nuclear PTC
- Production tax credit up to ~$15/MWh for existing nuclear units
- Phases down above ~$43-47/MWh power price
- Worth **$700-900mn annual EBITDA** to VST across the nuclear fleet
- Key legislative risk: any administration changes to IRA could reduce/eliminate this credit
- Under current law, persists through 2032+

### Federal/FERC Regulatory Developments
- FERC co-location proceeding: final order expected ~June 18, 2026; clears path for datacenter-adjacent-to-nuclear PPAs
- FERC interconnection queue reform: 2,600 GW backlog creates opportunity for VST's already-interconnected merchant fleet

### Root Causes — Five Factors

**1. AI power demand narrative cooldown (primary driver)**
VST was the poster child of the "power demand surge from AI" trade. When AI stocks (NVDA, hyperscalers) sold off in late 2025 / early 2026 on DeepSeek R1 and efficiency concerns, VST sold off in sympathy. Investors who owned VST as an AI infrastructure proxy reduced exposure.

**2. Company guidance deliberately below sell-side (perception gap)**
The company's FY2026 guidance midpoint ($7.2bn) was 15% below consensus ($8.4bn) as of the Q3 2025 earnings call. This looked like a miss even though management was being conservative about Meta PPA ramp timing and Cogentrix close.

**3. PJM co-location uncertainty**
The FERC proceeding on co-location rules created overhang on VST's ability to contract its remaining ~3.2 GW of nuclear capacity. Until those rules are clarified (expected June 18, 2026), Beaver Valley contracting discussions are on hold.

**4. ERCOT batching regulation**
Texas regulators debated how large AI datacenter loads should connect to the ERCOT grid, creating concern about near-term load growth assumptions. VST subsequently tempered its ERCOT load growth forecast from ~5-6% to **3-5% annually through 2030**.

**5. GS was Neutral until February 2026**
Goldman Sachs had a Neutral rating through February 5, 2026, which was an anchor on institutional buying. When GS finally upgraded on February 6 at $149.65, it provided a floor but the stock continued to drift lower.

## 7. VISTRA (VST) vs. GE VERNOVA (GEV) — SIDE-BY-SIDE COMPARISON

These are fundamentally different businesses that both benefit from the AI/power demand theme, but through completely different mechanisms.

| Dimension | **Vistra (VST)** | **GE Vernova (GEV)** |
|---|---|---|
| **Business model** | Owns & operates power plants; sells electricity | Makes equipment (turbines, transformers) + services them |
| **Revenue type** | Power price × volume sold | Equipment orders + long-term service contracts |
| **Customer** | Wholesale power buyers, retail electricity customers | Utilities, industrials, hyperscalers (equipment buyers) |
| **Key assets** | 41,000 MW generation fleet; 5mn retail customers | 7,000+ gas turbine installed base; $163bn backlog |
| **Revenue (CY25A)** | ~$17.5bn | ~$38.1bn |
| **EBITDA (CY25A)** | ~$5.8bn | ~$3.2bn (~8.4% margin) |
| **EBITDA margins** | ~33% | ~8-9% (Gas ~17%, Wind -4.5%) |
| **Valuation (CY26E EV/EBITDA)** | ~8.6x | ~39.3x |
| **FCF yield (CY26E)** | ~7.1% | Low (WC advances boost reported FCF) |
| **Leverage** | 2.4x Net Debt/EBITDA (FY2026E) | Net CASH of $8.8bn |
| **Capital intensity** | High upfront (plant ownership); low marginal cost for nuclear | Moderate (manufacturing); negative WC model funded by customer advances |
| **Growth driver** | Higher power prices, nuclear PPAs, AI load growth | Gas turbine orders, Electrification backlog, grid infrastructure |
| **Regulatory exposure** | ERCOT/PJM market rules, IRA PTC, FERC | Product safety, environmental, export controls |
| **Commodity risk** | High — exposed to natural gas prices, power prices | Low — equipment prices generally fixed at order |
| **AI exposure mechanism** | Direct: sells power to AI datacenters (AWS PPA, Meta PPA) | Indirect: sells turbines/transformers to utilities that power datacenters |
| **Moat** | Existing nuclear permits/interconnection irreplaceable; retail customer base | 7,000+ installed turbine base with long-term service agreements; only 3 manufacturers globally |
| **Key risk** | Power price decline, regulatory reversal of IRA PTC | AI demand deceleration, 43GW of SRAs (not firm orders) |
| **2025-26 stock** | ATH ~$220 (Sep 2025), now -37% at $138.54 | CMP ~$941 (Jun 5, 2026); UBS Buy $1,400, GS Buy $1,328 |

### The Key Distinction in Plain English

- **VST** benefits when power prices are **high** (it sells electricity)
- **GEV** benefits when capital spending on power infrastructure is **high** (it sells the equipment)

Both benefit from AI-driven power demand growth, but through opposite sides of the transaction. If AI demand surges, VST earns more per MWh sold; GEV books more turbine/transformer orders.

**GEV is capital-light, asset-light, with a negative working capital flywheel** (customers pay advances before equipment ships) — which is why it commands a massive valuation premium (39x EV/EBITDA). **VST is asset-heavy but cash-generative** at scale, and is far cheaper on valuation multiples (8.6x EV/EBITDA) because of commodity risk and leverage.

## 9. LONG-TERM BULL THESIS

### Power supply-demand: structural tightness
New build gas costs $2,500-3,000/kW → implies $100+/MWh LCOE. Current PJM energy + capacity ~$77/MWh. **At these economics, no new merchant generation is economically viable.** This means existing generation assets like VST's fleet have a structural moat — they can't be economically replicated. Meanwhile, data center load growth of 100 GW through 2030 is a reasonable estimate.

### Nuclear scarcity premium
VST has ~6.4 GW of nuclear — the only large-scale, 24/7, carbon-free generation in PJM and ERCOT. Only two other owners exist: Exelon (regulated) and Constellation Energy (pure merchant). The addressable market for nuclear PPAs is enormous (each of the major hyperscalers is seeking nuclear deals). With 3.8 GW signed and 3.2 GW remaining, VST has multiple shots on goal.

### FCF compounding machine
At $138.54 stock price, the implied FCF yields are:
- FY2026E: 7.1% (GS)
- FY2027E: 10.9% (GS); 11.6% (UBS)
- FY2028E: 13.1% (UBS)

Management is deploying FCF through dividends + buybacks (~$1.3bn annually) plus M&A (Cogentrix). At $22-25/share FCFbG by 2030 on today's ~$139 stock, the IRR to an investor is substantial.

### Credit upgrade as re-rating catalyst
VST at Ba1/BBB- is one notch below full investment grade on the Moody's side. An upgrade (expected 2026 per management) would: (1) lower interest costs, (2) expand the institutional investor base, (3) signal balance sheet strengthening.

## 10. KEY RISKS

1. **Power price decline:** ERCOT or PJM prices fall materially (large-scale renewable build, economic slowdown). Directly reduces merchant EBITDA.
2. **IRA PTC reversal:** Congressional removal of Section 45U would eliminate $700-900mn annual EBITDA floor.
3. **AI demand deceleration:** If hyperscaler capex slows, nuclear PPA pipeline stalls and the Helix thesis loses its engine.
4. **PJM co-location ruling unfavorable:** If FERC constrains co-location, Beaver Valley negotiations may need restructuring, delaying remaining 3.2 GW contracting.
5. **Cogentrix execution risk:** $5.5bn acquisition adding leverage before EBITDA ramp; PJM gas fleet faces capacity auction uncertainty.
6. **Leverage at closing:** Net debt/EBITDA could temporarily rise above 3x with Cogentrix close before PPA revenues ramp.
7. **Texas regulatory risk:** ERCOT batching rules could slow AI datacenter connectivity to grid.
8. **Interest rate sensitivity:** High leverage company in a higher-for-longer rate environment.

## 11. HISTORICAL CONTEXT — FROM BANKRUPTCY TO $220/SHARE

The 2018 UBS initiation puts the current story in perspective. In February 2018, VST had just emerged from the **Energy Future Holdings bankruptcy** (the largest US corporate bankruptcy at the time, filed in 2014). UBS initiated at $21/share (Neutral), valuing it at 7.1x 2019E EBITDA of $1.475bn.

**Eight years later:** EBITDA has grown ~4x to nearly $6bn. The stock peaked at ~$220. The transformation included:
- **Dynegy merger (2018):** Nationalized the fleet, added PJM capacity market exposure
- **Energy Harbor acquisition (March 2024):** Added ~4,000 MW nuclear + retail customers
- **Vistra Vision minority buyout (September 2024):** Bought remaining 15% minority interest in nuclear/retail sub
- **IRA PTC (2022):** Created a durable earnings floor for nuclear
- **AI demand wave (2024-2026):** Repositioned VST from "cheap commodity power stock" to "AI infrastructure company"

The 2018 UBS analysis was focused on whether VST could deliver Dynegy synergies at 7.1x EBITDA. The 2026 GS analysis values it at 10.5x EBITDA on a much larger base with a completely different growth narrative.
