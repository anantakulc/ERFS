## Executive Summary

**Recommendation: HOLD / WATCH — with a bullish bias conditional on three binary events**

Vistra Corp is a high-conviction bull story on paper — the largest competitive independent power producer (IPP) in the US, owning one of the scarcest nuclear fleets in North America, sitting in front of an AI-driven structural electricity demand shock, and carrying $6–7bn in incremental contracted revenue not yet in its guidance numbers. Against that, the stock has already de-rated 37% from its November 2025 high of $219.82, the balance sheet has deteriorated sharply over 24 months, and three regulatory binary events over the next 90 days could define whether the bull case is real or aspirational.

The core debate is not about the quality of the assets — it is about timing, leverage, and regulatory fragility. At $138.54, EV/2026E EBITDA is ~9x at the guidance midpoint, which is full-priced for a merchant IPP with no tangible book value floor. The 18-analyst consensus mean target of $225.29 (+62%) implies the market is severely mis-pricing the guidance-exclusion stack (Cogentrix + Meta PPA + Section 45U PTC). Kilo's bear case argues that two of five thesis pillars breaking simultaneously produces 40–50% downside to ~$80–100/share. The resolution of FERC co-location rules (expected ~June 18, 2026), the Cogentrix close, and Q2 2026 EBITDA are the three near-term arbiters.

Delta audit of numeric claims is flagged as a recommended follow-up given the volume and complexity of financial data in this cycle.

## Business Overview

### Company Profile
Vistra Corp (NYSE: VST) is the largest competitive IPP in the United States by installed capacity. Its ~41,000 MW fleet spans nuclear, natural gas, solar, and battery energy storage systems (BESS), serving ~5 million retail customers across 18 states. The company emerged from the bankruptcy of Energy Future Holdings in 2016 and has since consolidated aggressively, acquiring Dynegy (2018), Energy Harbor (2024, nuclear-heavy), and Cogentrix Energy (announced 2026-01-05, pending close).

## Bull Case

*Source: Charlie research thread*

### Argument 1: The Guidance-Exclusion Stack Creates a Durable Beat Runway

Vistra's official $7.2bn EBITDA midpoint for 2026 deliberately excludes three contracted revenue streams: Cogentrix (signed acquisition), Meta PPA deliveries (signed contract), and Section 45U nuclear PTCs (statutory entitlement pending legislative clarity). Each of these has a signed legal instrument behind it. The market appears to be giving limited credit to all three simultaneously. If any two of the three contribute as modeled, 2026 EBITDA prints above $8bn and 2027 approaches $10bn. Q1 2026 EPS of $2.90 versus $1.87 consensus (+53.4% beat) suggests the base business is already running ahead of street expectations before the exclusion stack is added.

### Argument 2: Nuclear Fleet Is Scarce, Long-Contracted, and IRA-Protected

Vistra's ~6,400 MWe nuclear fleet cannot be replicated on any reasonable construction timeline (15–20+ years, $10–15bn/GW). The Meta and AWS PPAs lock in ~3,800 MW at estimated rates of $85–100/MWh under 20-year terms — effectively converting a merchant commodity business into a regulated utility revenue profile for the contracted portion. The Inflation Reduction Act's Section 45U production tax credit (~$15/MWh for existing nuclear) adds a statutory floor that does not depend on power price levels. Approximately 59% of nuclear capacity will be locked under PPAs by 2034.

### Argument 3: Investment-Grade Upgrade Structurally Lowers Cost of Capital

On March 17, 2026, both S&P and Fitch upgraded Vistra to investment-grade (BBB-). This triggers automatic inclusion in IG bond indices, lien fallaway on secured debt (meaningfully reducing borrowing cost), and opens the company to a significantly larger investor base. The implied re-rating from merchant/high-yield to IG-regulated-utility-adjacent is not yet fully reflected in the EV/EBITDA multiple vs. peers such as Constellation Energy (CEG), which trades at a 3–4x premium on the same metric.

### Catalysts (Bull)

1. Cogentrix close H2 2026 — mid-single digit EPS accretion from 2027
2. Meta PPA first MWh deliveries (late 2026) — first revenue recognition from 2,600 MW contract
3. FERC co-location framework resolution — unlocks ~2,600 MW uncontracted nuclear for additional PPAs
4. Helix Digital Infrastructure first data center contract — signals $10bn fund operational
5. ERCOT summer 2026 heat spike — price optionality for uncontracted Texas generation
6. AWS Comanche Peak delivery start Q4 2027 — next PPA ramp-up

## Bear Case

*Source: Kilo research thread*

### Core Reason 1: Peak EBITDA May Already Be Behind the Company

FY2024 EBITDA of ~$6.87bn was the highest in VST's history. FY2025 EBITDA of $5.91bn was a -14% decline. The recovery to $7.2bn in 2026 guidance requires simultaneous execution of Cogentrix integration, PPA ramp, and favorable power pricing — none of which has been demonstrated at scale yet. FCF compression of 65% from 2023 peak ($3.78bn → $1.32bn) is structural, driven by capex escalation and interest expense growth, not one-time items. The 2026 guidance recovery is therefore not a return to trend — it requires flawless multi-variable execution.

### Core Reason 2: Balance Sheet Is a Ticking Constraint

Debt has grown 37% in two years ($14.7bn → $20.1bn). Net debt has grown 77% ($10.9bn → $19.3bn). Interest expense is up 73% ($0.60bn → $1.04bn). EBIT/interest coverage has compressed from 5.0x (2024) to 2.1x (2025). Tangible book value is negative at -$2.6bn. $4.2bn of debt matures within 12 months of December 2025. Cogentrix adds ~$1.5bn assumed debt plus $2.3bn cash outflow, pushing pro-forma net leverage to ~3.2x — above management's stated target of <3x. VST's debt/equity of ~355% compares unfavorably to NextEra (~120%) and Exelon (~150%). If 2026 EBITDA disappoints materially, the credit story rapidly becomes a liability rather than a catalyst.

### Core Reason 3: Regulatory/Policy Environment Is Actively Hostile on Multiple Fronts

- **FERC co-location (ruling ~June 18, 2026):** Prior FERC denial established adverse precedent. If co-located data centers must pay full transmission charges, the economics of the AWS and Meta PPA structures are impaired or require renegotiation. This is the single most time-sensitive binary event in the thesis.
- **Section 45U PTC:** While surviving the "One Big Beautiful Bill" House vote in May 2025, the path was not clean. House draft had accelerated sunsetting provisions; Senate Finance added uranium import restrictions; full IRA repeal remains on the offset menu in ongoing reconciliation. The $700–900mn/year contribution is not yet legally locked.
- **PJM capacity price collars:** FERC accepted an extension through 2029/2030 delivery years (April 28, 2026), capping capacity prices below what unencumbered market clearing would produce. VST explicitly opposed this extension. The cap suppresses ~18% of potential capacity revenue.
- **ERCOT battery saturation:** Battery storage revenue in ERCOT fell from ~$149/kW (2023) to a projected ~$17/kW (2025, per Enverus). Real-time prices fell $31/MWh YoY in 2024. With 15–18 GW of projected battery storage by end-2026 (from ~8.6 GW now), the Texas ancillary and energy arbitrage revenue stream that supported VST's Texas EBITDA is structurally impaired.

### Thesis-Breakers (Falsifiable)

1. FERC co-location ruling forces full transmission cost allocation on co-located data centers → PPA economics impaired → stock retests $132 low and breaks lower toward $100.
2. FY2026 adj. EBITDA prints below $6.5bn → net leverage exceeds 3.5x → credit watch → analyst cuts → re-rating toward ~$100/share (9x × $6.0bn bear EBITDA = $78–100/share range).
3. Section 45U modified or capped in any budget reconciliation → $700–900mn EBITDA gone → full re-rating from "clean energy infrastructure" to "merchant power commodity."

## Regulatory Section

### ERCOT (Texas)
Vistra is the largest generator in ERCOT. The Texas market operates without a capacity market — revenues are purely energy and ancillary. BESS saturation (from ~8.6 GW to 15–18 GW projected by end-2026) is compressing real-time price spreads and ancillary revenue. Bull counterpoint: nuclear baseload is largely insulated from real-time price erosion because it runs at full output continuously. Summer heat spikes remain a meaningful upside lever.

### PJM (Mid-Atlantic/Midwest)
Vistra's PJM exposure is primarily nuclear (Beaver Valley, Davis-Besse, Perry) plus acquired Lotus gas assets. PJM operates a forward capacity market (Base Residual Auction). FERC accepted the PJM capacity price collar extension through 2029/2030 delivery years on April 28, 2026 — suppressing capacity prices by an estimated 18% from unconstrained clearing levels. VST explicitly opposed this. The collar is a recurring headwind to East segment EBITDA estimates.

### FERC Co-Location
The most immediate regulatory binary: FERC is expected to rule on co-location frameworks for behind-the-meter nuclear PPA data center connections around June 18, 2026 — the day after this research cycle. Prior FERC action established adverse precedent by denying a co-location arrangement that bypassed transmission charges. If the ruling requires co-located hyperscaler data centers to pay full transmission tariffs, the effective PPA clearing price drops materially, and VST (and potentially its counterparties AWS/Meta) may need to renegotiate contract economics. A benign ruling — explicitly protecting existing behind-the-meter structures — is the single largest positive catalyst in the near term.

### Section 45U Nuclear PTC (IRA)
The Inflation Reduction Act Section 45U provides a production tax credit of approximately $15/MWh to existing nuclear generators (subject to income phaseout at higher power prices). For VST's ~6,400 MW nuclear fleet, this translates to roughly $700–900mn/year of after-tax economic benefit. VST has excluded 45U from its 2026 guidance as downside protection rather than base-case assumption. The credit survived the "One Big Beautiful Bill" House vote in May 2025 but faced amendment risk throughout. Any modification, cap, or phaseout in budget reconciliation eliminates a contribution equivalent to ~10–12% of current EV.

## Recommendation

**HOLD / CONDITIONAL BUY — Entry conditional on FERC co-location ruling outcome (~June 18, 2026)**

At $138.54, the risk/reward is asymmetric in both directions — a feature, not a comfort. The bull case to $225+ is credible and supported by 18 buy-side analysts with primary-source contracts behind the thesis. The bear case to $80–100 is equally credible given the leverage trajectory and regulatory exposure. The stock is essentially a binary straddling the FERC ruling.

Investors with a 12–24 month horizon and tolerance for 40% drawdown risk can initiate a position at current levels, sizing appropriately. Investors who require downside protection should wait for the FERC ruling before committing. If the ruling is adverse, the $132 52-week low becomes the first line of support, and a position there with a stop below $125 offers a better entry.
